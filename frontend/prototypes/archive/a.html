How it works

Every hour BasedNews reads the news feeds of 73 outlets in 10 countries and groups articles that report the same story, across languages. An AI judge then compares two articles about the same story at a time — which framing leans further left or right, more liberal or conservative — and a Bayesian model turns thousands of those comparisons into a position for every outlet, with an honest uncertainty.

01 Collect
RSS from the five largest news brands per country, plus extras. Robots.txt and opt-outs respected.
02 Match
Articles about the same event are grouped across languages with multilingual embeddings.
03 Compare
The judge sees two articles side by side, never the outlet's name, and picks one per axis.
04 Rate
A hierarchical Bradley–Terry model places outlets over their articles, with ovals of uncertainty.

Method

Everything is relative. The centre of the map is the average of the reference outlets, not a political centre. A country is the average of its five largest news brands. Inside a country view the centre is that country's own reference outlets; extra outlets are shown hollow.

Ovals are uncertainty. A large oval means few comparisons so far. Trails show where a dot has been: the lighter the line, the longer ago.

What is measured: how the same event is framed — not which stories an outlet chooses to cover.

Open data

The code, the prompts, every assumption and every experiment are public. Article text is never published: the site shows aggregates only. Running costs: about $3 a month for hosting and up to $5 for the AI judge.
