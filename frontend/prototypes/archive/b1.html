How it works

Every hour BasedNews reads the feeds of the five largest news brands in each of ten countries, plus a few more. Articles that report the same event are grouped together, whatever their language.

An AI judge is then shown two articles about the same event — never the outlets' names — and asked which one frames it further to the left or right, and which more liberally or conservatively. It does this thousands of times, choosing the comparisons that teach it the most per cent spent.

A Bayesian model turns those verdicts into a position for every outlet. Because each outlet is measured over many articles, a single unusual piece cannot decide where it lands.

Method

Positions are relative. The centre of the map is the average of the reference outlets, not a political centre. Open a country and its own reference outlets become the centre, with the smaller outlets around them.

What is measured is framing of the same event, not which stories an outlet chooses to cover. All prompts, assumptions and experiments are public, and article text is never republished.
